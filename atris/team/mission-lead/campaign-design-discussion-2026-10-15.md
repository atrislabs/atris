## Founder intent

- Keep Campaign → Mission → Task. Campaign creates missions; a member executes a simple mission directly or breaks a complex mission into tasks.
- Missions can exist without a campaign. Tasks are super-specific, atomic units of output, not vague activity.
- Show a member's active mission(s), associated campaign, and concrete work completed in a session. The structure should create continuity across sessions, with unfinished work preserved.
- For an open-ended campaign, the user cares: Do you understand what I want? Are we moving closer? Do you need anything from me?
- Useful progress communicates what was tried, what was learned, what happens next, and why. Receipts and validation support this underneath; the user should not manage those mechanics.
- The discussion should be captured in the project by the responsible member so it can be addressed later. Project first-campaign.md names mission-lead as the campaign's lead.

## Open design questions for Mission Lead

1. How should campaigns whose success cannot honestly be determined upfront by a script represent progress, learning, and human judgment? Measurable sub-results do not prove the broad subjective goal.
2. Who writes and maintains the durable progress narrative, where does it live, and how is it loaded next session? Avoid introducing duplicate reporting roles without need.
3. Reconcile the intended continuity with current HANDOFF.md wording that missions are one member/one run and end, while campaigns last. The conversation favored continuity of owned outcomes; do not silently change mission lifecycle based on this note.
4. Keep evidence distinct from completion: finishing atomic tasks does not necessarily fulfill a mission; fulfilling missions does not necessarily establish a broad campaign goal.

## 1. Topline

Ten open conjectures attacked across five rounds of an overnight fleet, zero refuted, and that is the expected outcome for problems that have survived decades of experts; the real product is a set of machine-checked frontier extensions that go past the published verification records. The solid new results: Seymour's second neighborhood conjecture is now exhaustively verified for all oriented graphs on up to 14 vertices with DRAT proofs checked by drat-trim (the published exhaustive record was 7 vertices), and to 15 with one short hand lemma; the Erdos-Gyarfas conjecture holds for every connected cubic graph of girth at least 6 on up to 30 vertices (a complete 122-million-graph sweep whose count matches the independently computed OEIS value exactly, with only 3 graphs avoiding both C4 and C8, each containing a C16) and girth at least 7 on up to 34 vertices; Hajos' cycle decomposition conjecture holds on all 367,860 connected 6-regular graphs and all 10,786 8-regular graphs on 13 vertices plus a fully closed margin-zero apex family, past the published n <= 12 line; R(5,5) = 43 gained an excluded-symmetry theorem (no counterexample coloring admits an automorphism of cycle type (43), (42,1), (21^2,1), or (14^3,1), hence no vertex-transitive counterexample exists); the Elphick positive square energy bound survived an exhaustive 12-million-graph sweep to n = 10; and Vizing's domination conjecture was proven with exact-arithmetic certificates on all 465 pairs from a pool built to maximize the known obstruction. Everything else is honest residue: two Hajos regular-graph sweeps and one 95-million-graph Erdos-Gyarfas sweep relaunched and still running at cutoff, hard SAT instances still grinding on the remaining R(5,5) symmetry classes, and the 11 giant circulant-weighing-matrix systems untouched by exact search. No result here bears on any conjecture beyond the stated finite ranges, and one lane (Seymour n <= 14) is the clear candidate for an actual write-up.

## 2. New verified results

Only machine-checked frontier extensions are listed. Each entry states exactly what was proven and what the verification rests on.

**Seymour second neighborhood, n <= 14 exhaustive with DRAT proofs (round 4).** Every oriented graph (no loops, no 2-cycles) on at most 14 vertices contains a vertex v with |N++(v)| >= |N+(v)|. Method: shard by minimum out-degree d0 (arc-count bound gives d0 <= floor((n-1)/2)), pin a vertex to out-degree exactly d0, add stabilizer-sound two-block lex-leader symmetry breaking; 57 UNSAT instances solved by CaDiCaL 3.0.1, every DRAT proof verified by drat-trim built from source. Extends to n = 15 via in-session Lemma T (the one remaining shard is forced to be a 7-regular tournament, where the equality |N++(v)| = |N+(v)| holds at every vertex). Independent cross-check: all oriented graphs on n <= 8 (575 million at n = 8) exhausted by a separate nauty geng/directg plus from-scratch C checker pipeline, totals matching OEIS A001174 exactly. Published exhaustive record before tonight: n <= 7. Also complete: all Cayley digraphs of all 55 groups of order 12 to 24 over all 1,505,160 admissible connection sets.

**Erdos-Gyarfas on high-girth cubic graphs (rounds 4-5).** Every connected cubic graph of girth >= 6 on at most 30 vertices contains a cycle of length 8 or 16, and every connected cubic graph of girth >= 7 on at most 34 vertices contains an 8-cycle. The n = 30 girth >= 6 class is 122,090,544 graphs (8 shards, total matching OEIS A014374, which was computed with the independent generator GENREG); 122,090,541 of them contain a C8; the 3 exceptions are C4-and-C8-free and each contains a C16 (each verified by two independent implementations, an exact Python DFS with cycle certificate and a C filter in verbose mode). The n = 34 girth >= 7 class is 1,782,840 graphs (matches OEIS A014375), zero C8-free. Full ladders below these sizes (girth 6: n = 20 to 28; girth 7: n = 24 to 32) also complete with counts matching OEIS. Generator completeness additionally supported by a canonical-form set-equality check between snarkhunter and geng at n = 24 girth 6 (all 7,574 graphs identical after nauty labelg). The cycle filter was validated bit-identically against the Python verifier on 13,168 graphs.

**Hajos cycle decomposition at n = 13 and beyond (rounds 1, 4-5).** Every connected 6-regular graph on 13 vertices (all 367,860; decomposition-size histogram {3: 193512, 4: 149029, 5: 24725, 6: 594}) and every 8-regular graph on 13 vertices (all 10,786, round 1) decomposes into at most floor((n-1)/2) = 6 cycles. The margin-zero apex family, K1 joined to every 5-regular graph on 12 vertices (degree sequence (12, 6^12), 7,849 graphs, class size matching the geng count of 5-regular graphs on 12 vertices), decomposes in exactly 6 cycles in every case. Also complete: all 540 10-regular graphs on 14 vertices, all 4,207 12-regular graphs on 16 vertices, all even circulants n = 13 to 16. Every heuristic decomposition was re-validated as an exact edge partition into simple cycles by an independently tested checker; zero CP-SAT escalations, so no result depends on solver trust; 43 random per-class samples independently decided by a pure-Python branch and bound with zero disagreements. Published verification line: n <= 12.

**R(5,5) = 43 excluded-symmetry theorem (rounds 1-5).** No 2-coloring of K43 avoiding monochromatic K5 admits an automorphism of cycle type (43) (full 2^21 circulant exhaust; minimum 43 monochromatic K5s observed, consistent with the orbit-size divisibility argument since 43 is prime), (42,1) (Cadical UNSAT at |H| = 42), (21^2, 1) (|H| = 21 UNSAT), or (14^3, 1) (|H| = 14 UNSAT). Corollary: no vertex-transitive counterexample to R(5,5) = 43 exists (43 prime forces a 43-cycle automorphism). Instance construction cross-validated by Burnside orbit counts. Caveat carried honestly: the UNSAT results rest on one solver and one encoding with no DRAT proofs retained; regenerating them with proof logging is the missing audit step.

**Elphick-Farber-Goldberg-Wocjan s+ >= n-1, exhaustive to n = 10 (round 1).** For every connected graph on 4 <= n <= 10 vertices (approximately 12 million graphs, per-order counts matching OEIS exactly), both s+ and s- are at least n - 1. Near-ties certified in exact arithmetic (sympy isolating intervals of width 1e-40). The closest known approach families (star plus pendant clique) have margins decaying like 1/n, verified positive to n = 3200, never crossing.

**Vizing's conjecture on 465 certified pairs (round 2).** For all 465 unordered pairs from a 30-graph pool constructed to maximize the integrality gap gamma minus gamma_f (the known necessary condition for a counterexample factor), gamma(G box H) >= gamma(G) gamma(H) is proven via exact-rational LP-dual certificates (closed-neighborhood packings re-verified in pure-Python Fraction arithmetic), zero solver trust. The one pair where the LP bound exactly equals gamma(G) gamma(H) = 16, circ12(1,4) squared, was settled strictly by an independent exact-cover search: no perfect code exists, so gamma >= 17.

**Circulant weighing matrices: systems proven empty (rounds 2-3, audited round 5).** No construction found for any open Strassler case. Exact coverage, audited in round 5 by diffing logs against regenerated system lists: 733 completed exact-DFS system runs, every one empty. Per target (systems complete-empty out of all m <= 45 cyclic/affine multiplier systems): CW(105,36) 114/124 counting the round-2 completion of the 1.3e9-candidate q = 4 system, CW(116,49) 28/30, CW(117,36) 85/95, CW(120,49) 171/178, CW(140,36) 166/189. The 11 untouched systems are exactly the deferred giants (8.9e9 to 1.84e16 candidates); their only coverage is heuristic (tabu, no hit). Sharding correctness was proven bit-identically on two known-solution cases, catching and fixing a real duplicate-emission bug. These are nonexistence results within the symmetry ansatz only.

## 3. Per-problem stories

**Seymour second neighborhood.** Round 1 pushed the exhaustive record to n = 11 but conditional on an unreviewed lemma and an unaudited SAT stack, and said so. Round 4 was the payoff: it audited the round-1 encoding (sound and complete), found the round-1 min-out-degree >= 4 lemma unprovable as stated (only >= 3 survives) and the n = 10 claim unevidenced, then made both moot by machine-proving all degree shards directly through n = 14 with verified DRAT certificates, double-proving n <= 8 by an independent enumeration pipeline, and exhausting all small Cayley digraphs. The n = 16 to 18 near-tournament shards were still running at cutoff. This lane went from weakest-evidenced to best-evidenced in the fleet.

**Erdos-Gyarfas.** Round 1 established by annealing that C4 and C8 are easy to kill in cubic graphs but C16 mass never approached zero, and named the exact plan: complete methods at n = 30. Round 4 built the generator and filter stack, validated it against published counts, and exhausted girth >= 6 to n = 28 and girth >= 7 at n = 30, 32; it also demonstrated (not assumed) that CEGAR SAT is a dead end here. Round 5 completed the n = 30 girth >= 6 and n = 34 girth >= 7 sweeps, found and doubly verified the 3 C4-and-C8-free survivors, and resolved a scary-looking generator mismatch as a labeling artifact via canonical forms. Remaining gap, stated plainly: at n = 30 the girth <= 5 classes are not covered (girth >= 5 alone is 14.6 billion graphs), so the published all-cubic frontier (through 29 vertices) is extended only within girth classes. An n = 36 girth >= 7 sweep (95 million graphs) is still running.

**Hajos.** Round 1 exhausted 6- and 8-regular n = 13 and mapped the tight regime (near-complete odd-n graphs, margin exactly 0, always decomposable). Round 4's agent died mid-run without reporting but left complete artifacts and a reproduction file. Round 5 harvested it: the apex margin-zero family fully closed (7,849/7,849 at exactly the bound), 6-regular n = 13 re-confirmed complete, 10-regular n = 14 and 12-regular n = 16 complete, toolchain re-verified with positive and negative controls, and the two dead sweeps (8-regular n = 14, about 3.46 million graphs; 10-regular n = 15, 805,579 graphs) relaunched from scratch with sentinel and tally scripts; both were about 2 minutes in when output was forced and continue detached. Partial sweeps were not counted as coverage.

**R(5,5) = 43.** Round 1 exhausted the 2^21 circulants and found the structural residue: the best colorings have exactly 2 monochromatic K5s, both arithmetic-progression shaped. Round 2 proved every widening of the repair neighborhood around every best local optimum UNSAT (237 free edges) and exhausted the first two multiplier symmetry classes. Rounds 3-5 grew the excluded-cycle-type list to four classes plus the vertex-transitive corollary, added a second independent code for the involution class, and got a strong solver-sanity receipt when a restarted subcube run reproduced four UNSAT levels bit for bit. The |H| = 7, 6, 3, 2 chain and the involution and ball instances are genuinely hard SAT and were still grinding at final cutoff. Six distinct 2-mono-K5 colorings are archived and independently verified.

**Circulant weighing matrices.** Round 1 reconstructed the exact 22-case open list, built the exact-integer verifier, and identified mid-size-orbit symmetric subspaces as the underexplored gap. Round 2 built the three-stage pipeline (orbit-spectral exhauster including the previously unsearched affine-multiplier variant, C branch-and-bound DFS, tabu) and validated it end to end by rediscovering five known matrices; it also proved the sign-multiplier ansatz empty for all CW. Round 3 added prefix-sharded DFS with proven-exact sharding. Round 5's audit found that round 3 delivered zero progress on the 11 giants it was created for and said so; the honest ledger is 733 systems exhaustively empty, giants heuristic-only. Everything is evidence of absence within the ansatz, nothing more.

**Gallai path decomposition.** Round 1 built two independent exact engines and exhausted n <= 8; round 2 ran about 1,140 further exact decisions across engineered dense families at n = 10 to 14, targeting exactly the regime no published theorem covers (dense, nonplanar, max degree >= 6). Every resolved graph decomposes within the bound; tightness is plentiful but brittle, and the two instances hard enough to time out CP-SAT were settled by Cadical at exactly the bound. The perturbation sweep of the 50 tight instances was 30 to 40 percent complete at its cutoff with a background watcher; no round 3+ lane picked it up.

**Vizing domination.** Round 1 validated the verifier and got about 200 pairs decided before cutoff, but produced the structural key: fractional domination makes small-integrality-gap factors provably hopeless. Round 2 rebuilt the pool around that obstruction and decided all 465 pairs with exact certificates, then attacked the true counterexample condition (survival slack) directly by hill-climbing and never got a qualified pair above -0.477. The round-2 structural finding, that raising the factor integrality gap forces the product LP bound up in lockstep, is an argument for why this search space cannot contain a counterexample.

**Elphick s+ >= n-1.** One round sufficed for the story: exhaustive to n = 10, the n = 11 sweep partial, and the near-tight families mapped exactly, with margins decaying like 1/n but provably positive as far as checked (n = 3200 numerically, n = 14 in exact arithmetic). The honest conclusion was that the bound looks true and asymptotically sharp, and a proof attempt (s- minus (n-1) >= c/n for graphs with a dominating vertex) is now worth more than more search.

**Barnette.** One round: exhausts at 44 and 46 vertices (about 9 million graphs, all Hamiltonian, all class checks passing) sit below the published frontier near 66 vertices, so this validated the pipeline without extending the record, and the report said exactly that. Solver-hard instances were archived as mutation seeds.

**Seymour, Hajos, EG round-5 relaunches and all still-running processes** are enumerated in section 5 with their sentinel files; any exit-42 or certificate file from those runs is a candidate counterexample requiring the lane verifier before belief.

## 4. What would publish

**Primary candidate: the Seymour n <= 14 DRAT frontier.** Doubling the published exhaustive record (7 to 14, 15 with Lemma T) with machine-checkable proof artifacts is a legitimate computational note in the style of prior verification papers. Outstanding audit before submission: (1) independent human review of the two meta-arguments that live outside the DRAT certificates, the degree-shard covering argument and the soundness of the two-block lex-leader symmetry breaking (written up in the lane's SOUNDNESS.md); (2) re-verification of all 57 DRAT proofs on a second machine and archival of the CNFs plus proofs; (3) review of Lemma T (short, but reviewed by no one); (4) optionally finish the n = 16 to 18 near-tournament shards. The independent n <= 8 double-proof and the OEIS count matches are already strong.

**Secondary: the Erdos-Gyarfas girth-class exhausts.** Every connected cubic graph of girth >= 6 on <= 30 vertices contains a C8 or C16 (with the 3 exceptional C4-and-C8-free graphs explicitly exhibited, each containing a C16) is a clean finite theorem extending the class frontier, and the 3 survivor graphs are independently interesting. Needs: a written generator-completeness argument (snarkhunter counts vs OEIS/GENREG plus the geng canonical cross-check), completion of the in-flight survivor-shard recount receipts, and honest scoping that girth <= 5 at n = 30 remains open. Worth a short note, possibly combined with the n = 36 girth >= 7 result if it completes.

**Worth a note with more audit: the R(5,5) excluded-symmetry theorem.** No vertex-transitive counterexample plus four excluded cycle types is a citable structural statement, but it currently rests on single-solver UNSAT with no retained proofs. Rerunning the four closed classes with DRAT logging and drat-trim verification would make it solid; the pending classes would extend it.

**Probably not standalone: Hajos n = 13.** Real frontier extension past n <= 12, but the soundness chain (heuristic plus independently tested checker, zero escalations) needs a careful write-up, and the natural completion (8-regular n = 14, 10-regular n = 15) is still running. Publishable as part of a broader verification paper once those close. Elphick n <= 10 and Vizing's 465 pairs are solid but likely below the novelty bar alone; the Vizing obstruction map might merit a remark in a survey. CW negative coverage is ansatz-relative and not publishable.

# Jack Dorsey

Co-founder of Twitter and Block. The current Atris-relevant read is his Block/Sequoia thesis: AI is not just a copilot layer; it can replace parts of hierarchy as the company's information-routing and coordination layer.

## Current Thesis

Dorsey and Roelof Botha argue that hierarchy exists because humans historically needed layers to route information across large groups. Their proposed replacement is a company organized as an intelligence: a company world model plus customer world model, with humans at the edge applying judgment, taste, ethics, trust, and craft.

Source note: refreshed from Sequoia's podcast transcript for the YouTube episode plus Block's co-authored essay. External links are stored in the local source receipt because Atris staleness checks treat `sources` as filesystem paths.

For Atris, the useful abstraction is:

```text
company artifacts + customer signal
  -> world model
  -> intelligence layer
  -> composed capabilities
  -> human edge judgment
```

## Organization Model

Block's target shape collapses traditional management into three roles:

- IC: builder/operator with agents and context, responsible for craft and execution.
- DRI: owner of a customer outcome or cross-cutting problem, responsible for accountability and strategy.
- Player-coach: builder who develops other people by doing the work with them, not by becoming an information relay.

Dorsey says Block was still early in the transition during the Sequoia episode: the depth from him to any individual was around five layers, with a target of two to three and an ideal of no reporting layers once the intelligence layer carries enough context.

## Product Implications

- Roadmaps should come from customer reality, not just product-manager hypothesis.
- When the intelligence layer cannot compose a solution because a capability is missing, that failure becomes a backlog signal.
- Capabilities are primitives; interfaces are delivery surfaces; the durable value sits in the model and intelligence layer.
- Money/transactions matter because they are high-fidelity customer signals, not just revenue events.
- A company that lacks a deep compounding signal risks using AI only as cost optimization.

## Leadership Notes

- CEO work shifts from managing reporting layers to designing the intelligence, setting intent, and checking alignment at the human edge.
- Dorsey names over-delegation inside Block as a regret because it made the company feel like separate holdings instead of one integrated financial network.
- He frames the current transition with humility: Block does not have it all figured out, and parts may break before they work.
- His durable CEO advice still centers on learning, judgment, focus, authenticity, logic, and empathy.

## Practices And Tools

- Vipassana meditation remains part of his focus and reactivity training.
- Block's AI-native work references internal artifacts, agents, prototypes, and machine-readable remote work streams.
- Prior wiki notes named Goose and Claude Code as relevant tools; current source refresh did not independently verify tool stack details.

## Atris Read

This is close to the Atris operating thesis, but the warning matters: intelligence only helps if the workspace has legible artifacts, strong customer signal, and a proof loop. Otherwise "AI-native company" can become a layoff narrative or a cost story instead of an operating system.

Atris should copy the useful parts:

- make work artifacts legible
- route through durable task state, receipts, and wiki memory
- turn missing capability into backlog
- keep humans responsible for taste, ethics, and final alignment

Atris should not copy the weak parts:

- vague AI-center rhetoric without verifiers
- pretending the model makes most decisions
- replacing management before the context layer is trustworthy

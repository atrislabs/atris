# Try 5: failure checklist

The idea was to have the worker silently check the answer for generic advice, hedging, missing math, invented facts, padding, and missed details. Quick exact asks still pass through unchanged. Version 2 added proportionate recommendations, decision-changing assumptions, and label-dependent instructions.

| Version | Skill wins | Plain wins | Ties | Skill scope growth |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 10 | 12 | 8 | 0/30 |
| 2 | 15 | 10 | 5 | 0/30 |

Both runs used the saved practice answers and tool setting `AMB_TOOLS=Bash,Read,Write,Edit`, required to match their recorded fingerprints. The second version is retained. This is a practice set used to tune the skill, not a blind final exam, and pass-through answers vary between runs.

Losses taught us:

- `emergency-fund-months` had incorrect opening figures despite correct later calculations. A check must catch contradictions in the answer itself.
- `kayak-buy-vs-rent` missed the practical risk that the gear and transport budget could erase savings. A calculation needs its consequential assumptions, not just arithmetic.
- `oncall-rotation-design` made a workable rule harder to follow with a confusing example. Checks and examples must earn their place.
- `photo-shoot-quote` omitted useful terms protecting the quoted price; `rental-car-insurance` missed decisive coverage checks and gave a questionable recommendation. Concision cannot replace the specific conditions needed for a decision.
- `protein-target-split` introduced confusing food portions; `piano-schedule-change` invented encouragement; `wedding-best-man-toast` put conflicting facts into the speech. Extra detail can undermine an otherwise useful answer.
- `pool-shock-dose` added an unsupported claim about a typical dose; `rsvp-headcount-formula` lacked a range that automatically includes future rows. Quick pass-through answers are not immune to ordinary answer variation.
